Hamilton basement, flood, and winter planning

Move the record before the object: document basement collections and winter access first

Hamilton’s preparedness guidance says emergencies can interrupt electricity, heat, water, and travel, and its flood guidance advises keeping important items and documents out of basements and photographing damage for insurance purposes. The Canadian Conservation Institute adds material-specific handling and storage guidance. For appraisal planning, treat a basement collection, storage locker, or weather-dependent route as an access and evidence question before treating it as a scheduling question.

Separate pre-event condition from later damage

Capture full views, surfaces, backs, bases, marks, labels, signatures, serial numbers, old repairs, and existing damage. Record dimensions, materials, storage level, receipts, family history, prior reports, and dated earlier photographs. Keep copies of the inventory and insurance records in a portable waterproof or off-site location, and do not discard or clean a wet or unstable object before seeking appropriate safety and conservation advice.

Define a safe inspection and transport plan

Confirm the exact address, floor, stairs, elevator, parking or loading route, item weight, handlers, and whether flooding, ice, snow, or a power outage changes access. Ask the recipient about intended use, value basis, effective date, inspection expectations, signer qualifications, and report format. Organize the evidence with the preparation checklist before anyone moves the item.
