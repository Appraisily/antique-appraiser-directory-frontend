Charleston flood, tide, and historic-collection planning

Check real-time access, keep a pre-flood inventory, and preserve historic context without overstating it

The City of Charleston’s flood GIS provides tide, road-closure, elevation, flood-map, and historical-flooding tools. Its floodplain preparedness guide tells residents to inventory and photograph contents and keep important papers elevated and dry. For appraisal work, that means the photo and document record should exist before a storm, the exact inspection route should be checked on the day of travel, and a historic Charleston address should be treated as a provenance lead rather than automatic proof about a movable object.

Create the pre-loss record above the water line

Photograph each item, its location, all sides, marks, labels, signatures, frames, bases, repairs, and existing water stains, corrosion, splits, or losses. Add measurements, receipts, family history, prior reports, and building or room inventories. Keep a secure copy and important originals away from basement, ground-level, or known water exposure so the evidence is available even when the property is not.

Make route and assignment scope explicit

Check tide, road-closure, flood, and evacuation information before anyone travels. Do not drive through standing water or enter a damaged structure to meet an appraisal appointment. Use the preparation checklist, document from a safe position, and tell the appraiser whether the request concerns pre-loss value, post-loss condition, insurance, estate, donation, or resale; each can require a different value basis and effective date.
