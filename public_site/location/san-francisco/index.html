San Francisco seismic and building-access planning

Secure the object, preserve the evidence, and map the building exit before an appraisal visit

San Francisco’s Earthquake Safety Implementation Program collects city, state, and federal guidance for securing contents, planning for disrupted services, and preparing homes and businesses before an earthquake. National Park Service museum guidance treats preventive care, documentation, handling, and emergency planning as connected collection responsibilities. For an appraisal, translate that into an item-specific plan: know what can fall, what cannot be safely moved, where the records live, and how an appraiser or handler would enter and leave the building.

Record support, attachment, and current condition

Photograph the whole object and its placement, then marks, labels, signatures, frame, mount, hanging hardware, base, joins, loose parts, repairs, and existing cracks or abrasions. Add dimensions, weight, material, provenance, receipts, earlier reports, and dated prior images. Do not unhang, disassemble, clean, or reposition a large or fragile item solely for appraisal photography without a safe handling plan.

Map high-rise, hill, and loading access

Provide the exact address, floor, elevator dimensions and booking rules, stairs, loading zone, parking limits, item path, required handlers, and any post-event access restriction. Ask the intended recipient to confirm purpose, value basis, effective date, inspection scope, signer qualifications, and report format. Use the preparation checklist and keep the inventory available off site.
