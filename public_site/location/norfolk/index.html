Norfolk tidal-flood inspection planner

Record condition and access before tidal flooding changes the evidence or makes an appraisal visit unsafe

Norfolk’s flooding information explains the city’s exposure to flooding, and its preparedness guidance directs residents to know local hazards, make a plan, and follow alerts. Applied to art, antiques, furniture, silver, books, and household collections, the useful decision is not whether every item needs an emergency appraisal. It is whether enough pre-event evidence exists to identify the item and distinguish longstanding condition from later water, salt, humidity, handling, or relocation damage—and whether local conditions allow a safe inspection at all.

Photograph the baseline and the route to the object

Record the item’s room and floor, overall dimensions, materials, marks, labels, serial numbers, known ownership, previous repairs, and existing condition. Photograph every side, the base or back, details, installation hardware, nearby floor and wall, and the route through doors, stairs, elevators, parking, or loading areas. Preserve invoices, estate inventories, prior reports, and dated room photographs off site. These records help a later reviewer separate identity and provenance evidence from assertions based only on where the item was found.

Use alerts and access as stop conditions

Before travel, confirm the address, tide or flood advisory, road and garage access, utilities, contamination, dry staging area, and two exit options. Never block an evacuation path with furniture or packing. After exposure, photograph from a safe position before cleaning or moving anything, and keep detached parts and labels associated with the correct item. Use the preparation checklist for remote triage; cancel the visit when warnings, standing water, unsafe electricity, contamination, or official instructions make access uncertain.
