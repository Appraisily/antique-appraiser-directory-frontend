Charlottetown heritage and storm-surge collection file

Use the heritage property search as a research lead and prepare collection evidence for outages, storms, and evacuation

Charlottetown provides heritage resources and a property-search path, and the City’s emergency guidance identifies weather-related risks including blizzards, ice storms, hurricanes, flooding, storm surge, and utility loss. A heritage record can establish facts about a building or protected place and may reveal names or dates worth researching. It does not authenticate movable contents. Emergency guidance supplies the operational boundary: preserve a compact off-site record in advance and do not turn an appraisal appointment into another task during an alert, evacuation, or prolonged outage.

Build the property-to-object evidence bridge

Record the exact property-search result and the object claim separately. Gather dated room photographs, bills of sale, probate or estate schedules, correspondence, shipping labels, exhibition or restoration records, maker marks, inscriptions, and measurements. Photograph the present room, full object, four sides where practical, back or underside, construction, labels, damage, and repairs before relocating it. Mark each statement as documented, reported by a named person, or still unknown so a reviewer can test the claimed association rather than repeat it.

Make the file portable before power or access is lost

Give every item an identifier and keep a copy of images, ownership papers, prior reports, and contact details outside the property. Note floor level, windows, exterior walls, basement storage, damp, corrosion, loose components, fragile finishes, packing, stairs, exits, and a dry staging area. Never clean, energize, unfold, or move a wet or unstable object merely for photographs. Use the preparation checklist for remote triage, monitor City alerts, and postpone travel when evacuation, storm surge, unsafe utilities, or blocked access applies.
