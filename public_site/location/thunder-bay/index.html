Thunder Bay severe-weather and object-care triage

Prepare a remote evidence package before severe weather or a risky move makes the object harder to assess

The City of Thunder Bay offers a severe-weather alert subscription, and the Canadian Conservation Institute publishes care guidance by object type. These sources support a practical local sequence: receive current official information, stabilize the situation, document the item, and then decide whether an in-person visit adds evidence. They do not identify a maker, certify a provider, or determine value. Their value is preventing avoidable handling and helping an owner preserve condition evidence when snow, cold, water, power loss, transport, or access constraints affect a collection.

Make the packet usable without a site visit

Give each item a stable identifier and record its room, floor, dimensions, materials, maker marks, serial or accession numbers, known ownership, and previous repair. Take an overall image, every side, back or underside, labels, inscriptions, fasteners, frame or base, condition details, and one image showing scale. Add receipts, inventories, prior appraisals, exhibition or restoration records, and dated family photographs. Store a copy away from the property and clearly distinguish documentation from unverified family history.

Use condition and access to choose remote or in-person work

Do not warm, dry, clean, polish, unfold, reattach, or transport a cold, wet, brittle, mould-affected, or unstable object solely to complete an appraisal. Photograph the surrounding environment and note condensation, staining, corrosion, lifting, distortion, detached elements, packaging, temperature change, and time of exposure. Check alerts, roads, power, stairs, lighting, loading access, and a dry staging area. Start with the preparation checklist; postpone direct inspection when official warnings or unsafe conditions remain.
