Edmonton inventory and climate planning

Document the collection before snowmelt, storms, smoke, or extreme temperatures complicate access

The City of Edmonton identifies extreme hot and cold temperatures, floods, summer and winter storms, and wildfire smoke among local hazards. Its preparedness guidance also recommends making a household inventory with photographs or video and keeping copies away from the home. For an appraisal, that inventory becomes the starting evidence—not a substitute for inspection, but a way to establish what exists, where it is stored, and what changed after an incident.

Record condition before transport

Photograph all sides, maker marks, labels, frames, bases, existing cracks, corrosion, staining, and storage conditions. Note whether an item must move through stairs, an elevator, an exterior loading area, or winter conditions. Review the Canadian Conservation Institute’s material-specific care guidance before cleaning, packing, or changing the environment.

Give the appraiser a workable scope

State the exact inspection address, item count, size and weight, intended use, recipient, value basis, effective date, deadline, and report format. Keep provenance, purchase records, earlier reports, and insurance schedules with the preparation checklist, with a secure copy outside the property.
