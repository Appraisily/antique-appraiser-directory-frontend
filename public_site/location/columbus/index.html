Ohio history, donation, and appraisal scope

Preserve an Ohio object’s context before choosing donation or valuation help

Ohio History Connection describes historic objects as primary sources and maintains catalog, documentation, storage, loan, and donation processes from Columbus. A museum’s collecting decision is different from a monetary appraisal: before contacting either, assemble the ownership history and explain whether you need collection care, institutional donation review, insurance documentation, estate value, charitable-contribution support, or resale advice.

Keep context with the object

Record labels, inscriptions, maker marks, dates, people and places connected to it, receipts, correspondence, prior exhibitions, repairs, and photographs of all sides. Do not separate an object from papers that establish provenance.

Ask for the correct deliverable

Name the intended user and value basis, whether physical inspection is required, who signs the report, how comparable evidence is documented, and whether the recipient has credential or timing requirements. A donation inquiry is not itself proof that an item will be accepted or appraised.
