Saskatoon provenance and collection-care planning

Use the city heritage record to strengthen provenance, then document condition before handling

The City of Saskatoon’s Heritage Register includes statements of significance for buildings, structures, monuments, memorials, and cultural landscapes, while the Canadian Conservation Institute publishes material-specific care guidance. Those resources do not establish an object’s value, but they can make an appraisal file more reliable: local names, dates, addresses, and historic uses can be checked against a public record, and fragile photographs, paper, furniture, paintings, or metalwork can be documented without creating avoidable new damage.

Separate an object story from verifiable provenance

Record the family account exactly as received, then look for corroborating addresses, owners, institutions, makers, inscriptions, dates, receipts, exhibition labels, and earlier photographs. A connection to a registered place can guide research, but it is not proof that an object came from that place. Preserve both the source and the uncertainty so an appraiser can tell documented facts from oral history.

Make the condition record before cleaning or moving

Photograph the whole item, front and back, construction details, marks, labels, frame or base, and every crack, stain, loss, repair, or loose part. Do not detach old photographs from albums, force stuck material, polish surfaces, or test a cleaner merely to improve appraisal photos. Use the preparation checklist, note storage conditions, and ask a conservator about unstable material before transport.
