Vancouver seismic and coastal planning

Prepare the collection for appraisal without overlooking earthquake, smoke, heat, or coastal-flood access

The City of Vancouver’s preparedness guidance directs residents to consider earthquakes, extreme heat, wildfire smoke, and coastal flooding and to plan for evacuation or sheltering in place. For antiques and art, that means recording the collection before an incident, knowing which objects are unstable or difficult to move, and giving the appraiser a realistic access plan for a condominium, hillside property, studio, warehouse, or off-site locker.

Stabilize the evidence, not the object

Photograph each item in place, then capture marks, labels, mounts, bases, cracks, loose elements, smoke or water exposure, and storage conditions. Record dimensions, weight, floor, elevator, stairs, loading route, and packer or conservator involvement. Consult Canadian Conservation Institute care guidance before cleaning, remounting, or changing storage.

Plan for jurisdiction and intended use

State whether the assignment is for insurance, estate, donation, family division, resale, or identification; name the recipient and required currency, value basis, effective date, signer, inspection mode, and report format. Keep provenance and earlier records with the preparation checklist, and confirm whether travel or remote review is appropriate.
