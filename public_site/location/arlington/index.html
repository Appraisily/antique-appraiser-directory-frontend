Arlington landmark-story and severe-weather evidence plan

Test an object’s claimed link to an Arlington landmark and preserve a pre-storm condition baseline

Arlington’s preservation material identifies local landmarks and historic districts, while the City’s flood-safety guidance places personal safety ahead of property and recommends advance evacuation planning. Those sources answer separate parts of an appraisal decision. A landmark address can direct research toward former owners, photographs, permits, or archives, but it does not prove that a movable artwork, furnishing, document, or collectible came from that property. Flood and severe-weather planning determine when evidence should be captured remotely and when an inspection must wait.

Turn “from this historic house” into a testable claim

Write down the exact address, room, owner, date range, and transfer being claimed. Then keep property sources separate from object sources. Photograph the item in place, every side, back or underside, maker marks, labels, inscriptions, construction, fasteners, repairs, frame or mount, and dimensions. Add dated interior photographs, estate inventories, receipts, correspondence, exhibition records, and restoration files. A city marker can support the building’s history; it cannot authenticate the item or establish its market value without an object-level chain.

Record the baseline before moving or sheltering property

Note the room and floor, existing cracks, stains, corrosion, lifting, loose parts, previous repairs, nearby windows or plumbing, and the route through doors, stairs, elevators, parking, or loading areas. Keep copies of the inventory and ownership records away from the property. Do not delay sheltering or evacuation to move a collection. Use the preparation checklist for remote triage, and schedule direct examination only after alerts, floodwater, electricity, contamination, and access conditions are safe.
