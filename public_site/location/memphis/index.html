Memphis severe-weather and access planning

Plan the appraisal around alerts, safe site access, and an item-level recovery record

The Memphis Office of Emergency Management coordinates local preparedness and monitors developing severe weather, while Shelby County’s preparedness guidance identifies earthquake, tornado, flood, and winter-storm risks. For an appraisal assignment, that local context changes the order of work: make the item record before a forecast event, confirm whether the property or storage site can be entered safely, and do not schedule transport through a warning, evacuation, flooded route, or power outage.

Create evidence that survives a disruption

Photograph the full object, every side, marks, labels, signatures, serial numbers, frame or base, repairs, and existing damage. Pair those images with receipts, family history, prior reports, measurements, and the exact storage location. Keep a secure duplicate away from the item so an appraiser can distinguish documented pre-event condition from later loss or damage.

Confirm the inspection path before anyone travels

Share the exact address, floor, stairs, elevator, loading route, parking limits, item dimensions, and whether the object can be moved without new damage. Ask the intended recipient about value basis, effective date, signer qualifications, inspection scope, and report format. Use the preparation checklist, and use remote triage when official alerts or access conditions make an on-site visit unsafe.
