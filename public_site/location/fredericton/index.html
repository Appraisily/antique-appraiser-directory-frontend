Fredericton flood-ready collection record

Create an off-site item record before flood conditions limit access, handling, or reliable condition evidence

The City of Fredericton’s emergency-preparedness material recommends planning ahead and keeping records for valuable possessions, while its flooding page directs residents to current flood and River Watch information. For an owner seeking appraisal help, the practical lesson is to capture identity, provenance, condition, and location before an event—not to move fragile or heavy objects during one. A pre-event record gives an appraiser a baseline; flood alerts and official access directions determine whether an appointment should proceed.

Build an off-site baseline that another person can understand

Give each item a short identifier and record its room, height, width, depth, materials, maker marks, serial or accession numbers, distinctive repairs, and known ownership history. Photograph the whole object, four sides where practical, back or underside, labels, inscriptions, frames, hardware, existing cracks, stains, losses, and how it is installed. Scan receipts, prior appraisals, inventories, restoration invoices, and family photographs. Store a copy away from the property and label uncertain family history as a claim rather than verified provenance.

Separate pre-event value evidence from post-event damage

If exposure occurs, do not clean, discard, plug in, unfold, or force apart an item solely for appraisal photographs. From a safe location, document water height, contamination, colour transfer, tide lines, swelling, warping, corrosion, mould-like growth, detached parts, and wet packaging. Keep the pre-event and post-event image sets dated and separate. Use the preparation checklist for remote triage, and wait for official clearance before any in-person inspection or re-entry.
