Kitchener flood, alert, and object-care planning

Match the item’s material risks to the route, storage site, and inspection plan

Kitchener’s emergency-planning hub links residents to regional alerts, flooding guidance, and the city response framework. The Canadian Conservation Institute organizes care advice by material—from furniture, paintings, books, and photographs to metals, textiles, plastics, and Indigenous collections. Before an appraisal, identify both the local access risk and the object’s physical vulnerability; a safe plan for a framed work may be wrong for a heavy cabinet, brittle paper, unstable metal, or a culturally sensitive object.

Document material, condition, and provenance

Take an overall image plus close views of joins, surfaces, backs, undersides, marks, labels, signatures, repairs, and active damage. Record dimensions, material, current storage level, ownership history, receipts, earlier appraisals, and any handling restrictions. Do not clean, unfold, disassemble, or move a fragile object simply to obtain a better photograph; ask first what evidence the assignment actually requires.

Set access and report requirements in advance

Confirm the exact municipality, address, floor, elevator, stairs, loading point, winter or flood access, item weight, and number of handlers. Ask the report recipient to specify purpose, value basis, effective date, inspection expectation, signer qualifications, and acceptable format. Organize the request with the preparation checklist and keep an off-site copy of the item inventory.
