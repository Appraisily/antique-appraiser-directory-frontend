London heritage-record and collection-care worksheet

Build a provenance packet from the property record without mistaking a heritage address for proof about the object

The City of London publishes a Register of Cultural Heritage Resources, while the Canadian Conservation Institute publishes object-care guidance for household and institutional collections. Together they give an owner two useful research lanes. The register may establish facts about a building, designation, address, or neighbourhood context. It does not establish who made a movable object, when the object entered the property, whether a family story is correct, or what market value applies. Conservation guidance helps document condition and handling risk before an item is moved or inspected; it is not an appraisal or authentication.

Create a three-column evidence chronology

In the first column, record only facts found in the city register or another dated property record. In the second, record object-specific evidence: invoices, probate schedules, exhibition labels, inscriptions, maker marks, restoration records, and dated room photographs. In the third, record oral history as an attributed claim rather than a fact. Photograph the object in place, then its front, back, underside, joinery, hardware, labels, damage, repairs, and measurements. This separation lets an appraiser test a claimed connection instead of inheriting it as an assumption.

Stabilize first and decide whether travel adds evidence

Do not clean, polish, tape, dismantle, reframe, or move a fragile item merely to improve photographs. Note loose elements, active flaking, mould, pests, water marks, unstable bases, and previous repairs, and keep packing materials away until risk is understood. Use the preparation checklist to send a complete remote packet. Ask for an in-person inspection only when scale, construction, condition, inaccessible marks, installation, or the intended user of the report requires direct examination.
