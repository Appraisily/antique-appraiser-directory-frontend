How We Build This Directory

Antique Appraiser Directory by Appraisily · Last reviewed July 28, 2026

This directory helps people find antique and fine-art appraisal providers by location, understand what evidence has been checked, and compare a local inspection with an online alternative when the assignment permits remote evidence.

What you’ll find here

  • Location pages that summarize appraisal needs (insurance, estates, donations, resale) and list local providers where available.
  • Dated provider profiles that state their publication status, source scope, and facts that still need direct confirmation.
  • Decision guidance and a reusable preparation checklist rather than the same generic paragraph repeated across every city.
  • Links for providers and readers to request inclusion, correction, or removal.

Verified and source-listed are different trust states

Verified

A human reviewer checked the displayed claim scope against the sources named on the profile and recorded a review date. Verification applies only to those listed facts; it is not a recommendation, employment relationship, or guarantee of current availability.

Source-listed

The profile has limited source support but has not passed the full manual claim review. A source-listed record must not be described as credential-verified, and its location, specialty, services, or qualifications may still require direct confirmation.

Unresolved identity, address, credential, or service-area conflicts are held from new public claims and discovery surfaces until a reviewer resolves them. Ratings, reviews, testimonials, prices, and credentials are never inferred.

How to read professional designations

Membership and professional designation are not interchangeable, and the relevant specialty matters. The directory records a designation only when a current official provider or association source supports the exact person, organization, level, and specialty.

  • ASA and AM: the American Society of Appraisers identifies AM as Accredited Member and ASA as Accredited Senior Appraiser. Its personal-property accreditation path distinguishes candidates from designated members. Review ASA’s official personal-property accreditation overview.
  • ISA, ISA AM, and ISA CAPP: the International Society of Appraisers publishes distinct Member, Accredited Member, and Certified Appraiser of Personal Property levels. Review ISA’s official credentialing levels.
  • Appraisers Association of America: the association publishes separate Accredited and Certified membership requirements for personal-property specialists. Review its official membership categories.

Ask the signer for a current qualifications statement and confirm that the designation, specialty, experience, intended use, and report scope fit the actual assignment.

USPAP and assignment-specific qualification

The Appraisal Foundation describes USPAP as generally recognized ethical and performance standards for the appraisal profession in the United States. Whether compliance is required depends on the applicable law, agency, client, credentialing body, and assignment. A page saying “USPAP” does not by itself prove that a particular signer, report, inspection method, or intended use is qualified. Read The Appraisal Foundation’s USPAP overview.

For insurance, estate, donation, litigation, lending, or equitable-distribution work, ask who will sign, what value definition and effective date will be used, which standards and jurisdiction apply, who the intended users are, and whether physical inspection is required.

Fees and independence

Providers may publish hourly, flat, per-item, or project fees. The directory displays a fee only when a dated public source states it. Confirm what research, inspection, travel, photographs, revisions, comparables, and report delivery are included before signing an engagement.

Avoid compensation that depends on the appraised value, the desired conclusion, or a future sale outcome. If the same business also buys, sells, consigns, brokers, or manages property, ask for the appraisal role, disposition role, compensation, and potential conflicts to be disclosed separately.

Local inspection or online evidence?

Choose a local provider when the assignment or client requires physical inspection, or when scale, construction, condition, marks, authenticity, or materials cannot be documented reliably from photographs. Remote evidence can be practical when the assignment permits it and the item can be documented clearly.

Use the art-appraiser decision guide or antique-appraiser decision guide to compare the paths. Confirm the inspection decision with the appraiser and intended user before engagement.

Printable appraisal preparation checklist

Prepare this evidence before contacting a provider. It helps the appraiser assess fit; it does not guarantee that remote evidence will be sufficient.

  • Overall front, back, side, top, and bottom photographs
  • Close-ups of signatures, labels, hallmarks, serial numbers, and inscriptions
  • Condition, damage, repairs, restoration, and missing-part photographs
  • Measurements, weight where relevant, materials, and construction details
  • Artist, maker, model, date, and attribution information
  • Receipts, invoices, catalog pages, and prior appraisal reports
  • Provenance, ownership history, exhibition history, and family documentation
  • Conservation, restoration, framing, and insurance records
  • Number of items and whether similar items form a collection or group
  • Intended use: insurance, estate, donation, division, sale, lending, or another purpose
  • Required effective valuation date, delivery date, and intended users
  • Any client, insurer, attorney, accountant, court, or agency instructions

Evidence review and corrections

  1. We search for the exact provider or location and prefer official business and professional-association sources.
  2. Each accepted claim records its source URL, source type, retrieval date, paraphrase, confidence, display scope, and reviewer status.
  3. Conflicts fail closed: pending claims do not enter public HTML, city cards, feeds, or sitemaps.
  4. Public profiles state the source scope and last-reviewed date; readers should still confirm current facts directly.
  5. Correction requests are reviewed against a source before public facts or trust state change.

We avoid publishing unsupported review counts, ratings, testimonials, fees, turnaround promises, credentials, or service areas in visible content or structured data.

Material publication-state, routing, and verification-policy changes are recorded in the directory verification updates.
